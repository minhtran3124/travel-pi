# MIN-59 — Runtime Rubric Grader: latency / cost / quality trade-off

Generated by `eval/run_grader_ab.py` over the MIN-58 eval set (19 cases: 15 graded, 4 control).

## Methodology

- Each case runs **twice** through the real compiled graph, fresh `thread_id` per
  (case, arm), grader **off** then **on**.
- **Arm lever:** `AgentContext(allow_grader=...)`, passed directly to `graph.invoke`.
  The production lever is the DB row `agent_config."grader".enabled`, but that is read
  by `_resolve_context` on the HTTP path only — this harness invokes the graph directly,
  so the DB row has no effect here. The flag is the same one the router would produce.
- **OFF is not a no-op path:** it still traverses `sub-agent → grader → reflection`
  (the grader delegates without calling its judge). A small constant offset in the OFF
  arm is that extra node hop, not grader cost.
- **Cost unit** = chat-model invocations, counted with a LangChain callback, not tokens.
- **Quality** = MIN-58 G-Eval rubrics, each applied only to the cases that declare it
  (`metrics` field). MIN-58's `no_hallucination` is **replaced** by a `grounding` rubric
  that receives the run's own tool output as `retrieval_context` — without it the judge
  cannot tell an invented hotel from a real one, which is the axis the grader moves.
- Judge = `gpt-4o-mini`. Scores are LLM-judged and **not** perfectly reproducible;
  single-run deltas on a 19-case set are directional, not statistically significant.

## Results

### Cost and latency (graded flows only)

| Measure | Grader off | Grader on | Delta |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mean LLM calls / turn | 4.27 | 5.80 | +36% |
| Mean wall-clock / turn | 6.3s | 10.6s | +68% |

**Revision-trigger rate:** 9/15 graded turns (60%) had the grader ask for at least one revision.

`needs_revision` is a **transient** verdict — it is the state that sends the sub-agent back for
another draft, so it is never the verdict a turn *ends* on. The turn terminates on `satisfied`,
`max_iterations_reached`, or `grader_error`. The revision-trigger rate above is therefore counted
from `grader_iterations > 0` (how many turns saw at least one `needs_revision`), not from the
terminal-verdict table below.

| --- | --- |

| `max_iterations_reached` | 3 |

## Grader false-positive spot-check

All 8 revised cases were read by hand (`off.reply` vs `on.reply` in `ab-results.json`).
**The `grounding` gain above is mostly an artifact.** The grader raises the grounding score by
pushing the agent to *refuse to answer*, not by making it more accurate. The judge rewards a
refusal — it invents nothing — while the traveller gets nothing.

| # | Case | Δ grounding | What actually happened |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Tìm khách sạn ở Đà Nẵng | +0.13 | **Real win.** Dropped the hotels no tool returned, kept the useful shortlist. |
| 2 | Compare Hanoi and HCMC | +0.06 | Mild. Hedges into "What I can say confidently", still useful. |
| 3 | List top things in Da Nang | −0.02 | Neutral. |
| 4 | Gợi ý món ăn nên thử ở Huế | +0.72 | **Abstention.** "Mình chưa tìm được dữ liệu món ăn… không muốn bịa." |
| 5 | Mang gì khi đi Sa Pa mùa đông | +0.43 | **Abstention.** "Chưa lấy được gợi ý đóng gói… thử hỏi lại sau." |
| 6 | What should I pack for Nha Trang | +0.85 | **Abstention.** OFF gave a genuinely good packing list; ON says "I couldn't retrieve the packing suggestion right now." |
| 7 | Break down a daily backpacking budget | −0.35 | **Abstention + format collapse** (0.89 → 0.31). Refuses to give a budget because the tool returned only general notes. |
| 8 | Tìm khách sạn ở Hội An | −0.32 | **Damage.** Two revisions garbled the answer: `**4.9★**, **4,**` and `**Khách sạn thứ 3** — trong kết quả tìm kiếm`. The OFF answer was already correct (0.99). |

**Score: 1 clear win, 1 mild win, 1 neutral, 4 abstentions, 1 outright regression.**

### Why

The `grounding` criterion in `prompts/grader_concierge/v1.md` says *"Every hotel, place, price,
and fact in the answer comes from a tool result."* Applied to a hotel listing that is exactly
right. Applied to *"what should I pack for Sa Pa?"* it means the concierge may not use its own
travel knowledge — so it stops answering. `GRADED_FLOWS` contains the whole `concierge`
sub-agent, but the concierge also answers packing, food, budget, weather, visa and currency
questions. The original request scoped this to **hotel search / booking**; widening it to the
whole sub-agent is what produced the abstentions.

Case 8 is the true false positive: an already-grounded answer was revised twice into a worse one.
`max_iterations_reached` there is a quality signal, and it is negative.

### Two further observations

- **`grader_error` = 1/15 graded turns (6.7%).** The cheap judge occasionally emits JSON with
  trailing characters (`ValidationError: Invalid JSON: trailing characters`). Fail-open worked —
  the traveller's reply was preserved — but 6.7% of turns get no grading at all. This is risk R4
  from the plan, now measured.
- **Cost is not uniformly negative.** When the grader is `satisfied` on the first pass it
  *replaces* reflection's judge call rather than adding one, so those turns are **cheaper** than
  baseline (e.g. `What hotels are available in Hanoi under $50`: 5 → 4 calls). The +36% mean is
  driven entirely by the 60% of turns that trigger a revision.

## Recommendation

**HOLD. Do not enable in production, and do not expand to other sub-agents.** The prototype has
demonstrated one thing convincingly and one thing negatively:

1. ✅ **On hotel search — the flow the request actually named — the grader works.** It caught a
   real hallucination live (a first draft invented `Merry Hotel Danang` and `Hilton Da Nang`;
   neither was in the tool output) and case 1 above reproduces the effect offline.
2. ❌ **On the rest of `concierge`, the rubric is actively harmful.** It converts useful advice
   into refusals, and once it can rewrite an answer twice it can degrade a correct one.

### Before this can ship

- **Narrow the scope.** Either drop `concierge` from `GRADED_FLOWS` and gate on the *turn* (did it
  call `search_hotels` / `browse_hotels`?), or split the rubric so `grounding` applies only to
  tool-sourced entities — hotel/flight/place names, prices, ratings, times — and explicitly
  permits general travel knowledge for advice questions. The rubric must say what the agent *may*
  invent, not only what it may not.
- **Re-measure with an abstention guard.** The offline `grounding` rubric cannot distinguish
  "accurate" from "said nothing". Add a helpfulness/answer-completeness rubric alongside it, or
  the next A/B will report the same false positive.
- **Fix the degradation path (case 8).** A revision that lowers the score should be discarded, not
  shipped. Today the last draft always wins.
- **Reconsider `max_iterations = 2`.** Every damaging case used the full budget. One revision
  captured most of the benefit (cases 4–6 were `satisfied` after a single round).

### What the numbers say if you only read one line

Grounding on genuinely-revised cases: **+0.188** (n=8) against a **0.104** noise floor — but a
hand-read of those 8 shows the gain is 4 refusals, 1 regression, and 2 real improvements. The
statistic is real; its interpretation is the opposite of favourable.

### Caveats

- 19 cases, one run, LLM-judged. Directional, not statistically significant.
- Both arms re-invoke the agent, so non-revised cases differ by sampling alone; that is the
  noise floor quoted above, and it is large relative to the effects.
- Latency was measured against a live OpenAI endpoint and includes network variance.
- The judge (`gpt-4o-mini`) and the graded agent share a provider; correlated failure is possible.
